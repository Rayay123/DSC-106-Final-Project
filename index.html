Two Climate Futures

Mapping the warming gap between a low and high-emissions world by 2100

Don Le · Raynard Taneka · Ethan Lam

The question

How much more warming does a high-emissions future produce than a low-emissions future, and where is the difference largest?

Pick a place

The map compares the projected warming gap near the end of the century. Choose one of the locations or click a map square to see how the three emissions scenarios change over time.

What stands out

The scenarios do not separate evenly everywhere. By the end of the century, the gap between low and high emissions is especially large in some polar regions, while places closer to the equator generally show a smaller gap.

Prototype progress

What have we done so far?

We started by narrowing the project to one question about how the warming gap changes across locations. We prepared a smaller browser-ready version of the CMIP6 temperature data for the low, middle, and high emissions scenarios. We built a clickable world map that updates a line chart when the reader chooses a location. We also added direct values, a color legend, preset locations, and an optional y-axis adjustment so the different places are easier to explore.

What will be the most challenging part to design, and why?

The hardest part will be showing local differences without making the data look more precise than it is. Each map square covers a large area, so a square near a coast can include both land and ocean. We also need to keep the chart scales fair across locations while still letting readers notice smaller local changes. Finally, the page must explain that these values come from one climate model and represent possible futures rather than exact forecasts.

About the data

The prototype uses monthly CMIP6 projections from the NASA GISS-E2-1-G model. Warming is measured from the 2015–2024 average and summarized with a ten-year rolling mean. The results show one model run, so they do not represent the full range of uncertainty across all climate models.
